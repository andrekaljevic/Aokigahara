1. Scope, method and limits
This PDF consolidates the full Aokigahara work produced across the conversation: visual sourcing,
official mapping, geology, terrain, vegetation, caves, routes, sector planning, evidence-density
scoring, temporal aerial layers, boundary separation and the machine-readable outputs used to
organise the reconstruction.


    It is not evidence of a literally perfect digital twin. Broad geography, lava geology, principal routes/caves
    and many visual conditions are well constrained. Exact tree placement, deadwood, local undergrowth,
    obscure footpath geometry and fine micro-topography in poorly photographed interior cells remain partly
    inferred.




Research hierarchy
     1. Geology first: GSJ eruptive-unit mapping and GSI volcanic landform data define the substrate.
     2. Terrain second: GSI bare-earth DEM/LiDAR products recover the physical surface hidden by canopy.
     3. Modern surface third: current aerial/vegetation/road data constrain present canopy, edges and
        infrastructure.
     4. Ground truth fourth: geolocated and route-continuous images validate appearance and scale.
     5. Viewpoint validation: macro camera matches at Sankodai, Koyodai, Lake Sai and other anchors test skyline
        and landscape alignment.


    Visual-file note. The original research workflow indexed and hot-linked external image sources; it did not
    locally redistribute every original photograph. Accordingly, this PDF contains the complete 152-entry linked
    visual/map/GIS catalogue and preserves the full manifest as an embedded attachment. The externally hosted
    original image binaries themselves are not repackaged here.

2. What each research pass added
   Pass 1 - visual and geography foundation
   Initial dossier assembled 109 still-image references, one motion reference and 13 dedicated map/GIS/geology
   sources. The central finding was that the named forest, the Jogan lava field, protected-area boundaries and the
   practical reconstruction extent must not be treated as one boundary.



   Pass 2 - reconstruction blueprint
   The research was reorganised into a ten-sector build plan, cave scale anchors, terrain/geology rules, vegetation
   communities and a confidence framework. The indexed asset base rose to 122.



   Pass 3 - measurable spatial coverage
   A 250 m grid over a deliberately broad research AOI produced 2,790 evidence cells. The first strict pass classified 81
   as high evidence, 1,283 medium and 1,426 low. A GSI terrain acquisition plan catalogued 3,528 candidate DEM tile
   URLs, and the first georegistration-control register was created.



   Pass 4 - modern vegetation, topology and source audit
   The Ministry of the Environment Existing Vegetation Map 2024 was added as the current vegetation-class layer,
   with underlying polygon source years retained. Route topology was separated from route geometry and access
   status. An authoritative-source register and a live reconstruction atlas were added.



   Pass 5 - time-series aerial evidence and boundary separation
   The atlas was expanded with historical GSI aerial series from 1961-69 through current imagery, Fuji volcanic
   terrain/geomorphology layers and the GSJ 2016 Fuji Volcano Geological Map WMTS. A dedicated boundary
   register separated research AOI, canopy, Jogan lava, Natural Monument, national-park, administrative and forest-
   management geometries. The final manifest in this master build contains 152 visual/map/GIS entries and 22
   authoritative-source records.

5. Geology and terrain
The terrain is a lava landscape. Research in the Motosu sector distinguishes major Jogan/Aokigahara flows including
Ishizuka Lava and Nagaoyama Lava. Work on the Motosu front described roughly 36 protruding lava lobes,
together with pinching, swelling, branching, clefts and clinker-rich surfaces. Those structures should drive the
environment's ground geometry and material masks rather than be replaced by generic procedural woodland noise.




Coordinate and terrain pipeline
Project logic: use a metric Japan Plane Rectangular Zone VIII grid for Yamanashi, retain WGS84 for interchange, and
normalise every source datum at ingest. The working blueprint identifies EPSG:6676 (JGD2011 / Japan Plane
Rectangular CS VIII) as a stable documented project CRS, but GSI's current download services may use JGD2024.
Mixing generations silently would create metre-scale alignment errors.

     • Check GSI 1 m DEM coverage first; if unavailable for the required mesh, use 5 m DEM5A airborne-laser data
       where available, then DEM5B/5C photogrammetry, with 10 m as fallback only.
     • Generate bare-earth terrain first; GSI DEM removes trees/buildings from ground surface.
     • Overlay GSI Fuji volcanic-landform classification and GSJ geology; preserve lava fronts, lobes, clefts,
       depressions and flow breaks instead of smoothing them into generic forest ground.
     • Treat photogrammetric/aerial canopy as a separate surface/vegetation validation layer, not terrain elevation.




Figure 2. Candidate terrain acquisition plan. Candidate URLs are not proof that the corresponding high-resolution DEM exists in every cell.




Geological source priority
The preferred detailed geology source is the Geological Survey of Japan's Fuji Volcano Geological Map, 2nd edition
(2016). Its vector Shapefile source is the correct future production input for isolating the Jogan/Aokigahara eruptive
units. The live WMTS is useful for visual overlay and QA but should not replace vector ingestion where exact polygon
geometry is needed.

6. Vegetation and forest structure
A uniform 'dense conifer forest' biome would be wrong. A University of Tokyo survey distinguished seven forest-
community types used in the blueprint:


     open Pinus, Pinus, Tsuga-Pinus, Tsuga, Chamaecyparis-Tsuga, Chamaecyparis, deciduous broadleaved


     • Tsuga forest occupies a large part of Aokigahara in the Tokyo University survey.
     • Chamaecyparis-Tsuga and Chamaecyparis forest occur toward higher elevations in that survey.
     • Deciduous broadleaved forest is associated with deeper-soil sites that escaped lava cover.
     • Open Pinus is associated with exposed lava/disturbance sites; do not scatter all seven types uniformly.

The modern spatial layer is the Ministry of the Environment Existing Vegetation Map 2024. Its underlying polygon
observations span 1999-2023; the polygon creation/source year must be retained rather than presenting all
vegetation as surveyed in 2024. Current GSI orthophotography should then be used to detect later clearing, road-
edge and canopy changes.
