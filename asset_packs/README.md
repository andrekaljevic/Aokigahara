# Photo-derived 4K asset packs

One pack per reference photograph. Each pack turns a single photograph into the things a
look-development pass needs from it: tileable 4096 × 4096 PBR texture sets for **bark**,
**greenery** and **ground**, a 4096 × 2048 **sky** dome fitted with the viewer's own sky shader, a
**lighting** preset in the exact shape of `PRESETS` in `viewer/app.js`, and a **mood** record
(dominant palette, tonal statistics, a `.cube` colour grade). A 4096-wide contact sheet per pack
shows all of it at a glance.

Everything is generated by `construction_code_v2/gen_asset_packs.py` from
`construction_code_v2/asset_packs_config.json`, deterministically, with no network and no
machine-learning model. Every number that went into a pack, and every assumption behind it, is in
the pack's `<pack>_manifest.json`.

## What is in a pack

```
<pack>/
  <pack>_manifest.json        provenance, colour handling, regions, parameters, checksums, limits
  <pack>_sheet_4k.jpg         contact sheet: source with regions, palette, lighting, sky, tiles, grade demo
  bark/ greenery/ ground/     <material>_diff_4k.jpg     sRGB albedo, large-scale shading flattened
                              <material>_nor_gl_4k.jpg   OpenGL tangent-space normal (project convention)
                              <material>_rough_4k.jpg    roughness, heuristic per material class
                              <material>_height_4k.jpg   height proxy from luminance bands, dark = low
  sky/                        <pack>_sky_equirect_4k.jpg 4096 × 2048 equirectangular dome (LDR, sRGB)
                              sky.json                   sampled sky colours by elevation, fitted parameters
  lighting/                   lighting_preset.json       preset + measurements + derivation
                              preset_snippet.js          one line ready to paste into PRESETS
  mood/                       mood.json                  palette, tonal statistics, grade parameters
                              grade_33.cube              33³ colour grade, display-referred sRGB in/out
_sources/                     the five owner-supplied photographs, byte-identical to the uploads
PACKS_MANIFEST.json           index of every pack
```

The map layout (`diff`, `nor_gl`, `rough`, `height`) is the one the runtime already uses for
`viewer/assets/materials_v2/`. Ambient occlusion is not shipped; it is
`1 - k·max(0, blur(height) - height)` at two radii if wanted.

## How the textures were made

1. **Colour.** The phone photographs carry a Display P3 profile; they are converted to sRGB with
   LittleCMS before anything else. The stock preview has no profile and is taken as sRGB.
2. **Region.** Each material comes from one hand-placed quadrilateral (normalised coordinates in
   the config). The quad is perspective-rectified. For trunks whose both silhouettes are visible
   the rectified strip is additionally unwrapped as a cylinder (±78°), so plates near the edges are
   not compressed. Grazing-angle ground regions are stretched vertically; they are not top-down
   captures and the manifest says so.
3. **Illumination flattening.** Each channel is divided by its own large-scale mean, which removes
   sun-side/shade-side gradients and colour drift while keeping texture-scale variation. The
   photograph's illuminant and white balance are **not** neutralised: a tile made under evening
   sun stays warm.
4. **Scale.** The region is enlarged towards the physical size a tile of its class should cover
   (2 m for bark, 1.5 m for moss, 3 m for canopy, …), never more than 3×. The manifest records
   both the tile's physical width and the source's real texel density; detail above the source
   density is interpolation. Enlarged sources get a documented 3 % periodic micro-grain.
5. **Synthesis.** Efros–Freeman image quilting on a torus: exhaustive overlap search over every
   window of the source (and its mirror where the texture has no fixed direction), minimum-cost
   seams, and a reuse penalty so a small source does not repeat one feature on a lattice. The
   result tiles seamlessly in both axes. The horizontal cuts are constrained at patch corners so
   that no patch keeps old content on both sides of its neighbour's freshly pasted corner; without
   that rule a straight edge survives at the end of every overlap band, which
   `tools/verify_asset_packs.py` now checks for.
6. **Derived maps.** Height is a mix of three luminance bands (finest at 6 px on the 4K tile, so
   JPEG noise does not become relief); normals follow the project's `height_to_normal`
   convention with periodic differences; roughness is a per-class base modulated by fine
   luminance. None of these is a scanned surface.

## Sky, lighting, mood

- **Sky.** Sky pixels are found inside a region of interest by colour, brightness and local
  smoothness, given an elevation from a 35 mm-equivalent focal length and a camera pitch estimate,
  and fitted with a NumPy port of `viewer/vendor/addons/objects/Sky.js` (Preetham) followed by the
  renderer's ACES filmic tone mapping. Turbidity, Rayleigh, Mie, the camera-relative sun azimuth
  and a nuisance exposure are fitted. The dome is rendered with the viewer's fixed sun azimuth
  (155°) and the pack's estimated sun elevation, in the `EquirectangularReflectionMapping`
  convention (top row = zenith). Where no clean sky is visible (the print, the park, the stock
  preview) the manifest says so and no dome is written.
- **Lighting.** A probe region is split into lit and shaded parts by large-scale illumination,
  not by texture. The key-to-fill ratio maps to `sun` and `hemi` with a rule calibrated so the
  three existing presets are reproduced from their implied ratios; `hemiSky` comes from the
  visible sky or from the probe's shade/direct tint, `hemiGround` and `fog` from probe means with
  their luma matched to the existing presets. The direct-light tint (albedo cancelled) and a
  McCamy colour temperature are reported for reference only; the viewer keeps `sun.color`
  fixed.
- **Mood.** k-means palette with role labels, luma percentiles, saturation, hue histogram,
  shadow and highlight tints, and a parametric grade (black/white points, gamma to the median,
  split toning with the measured tints, saturation) written as a `.cube` LUT and previewed on
  `renders/after_p4/C1_start_corridor.png` in the sheet.

## Using them in the viewer

Add a preset: paste the line from `lighting/preset_snippet.js` into `PRESETS` in `viewer/app.js`
and add an `<option>` to the `#light` select in `viewer/index.html`.

Use a dome as environment or background:

```js
const tex = new THREE.TextureLoader().load('../asset_packs/oak_lichen_evening/sky/oak_lichen_evening_sky_equirect_4k.jpg');
tex.mapping = THREE.EquirectangularReflectionMapping; tex.colorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace;
scene.environment = tex;   // or scene.background = tex
```

Apply a grade with `LUTCubeLoader` + `LUTPass` from `three/addons/` on the tone-mapped frame.

Use a texture set exactly like `materials_v2`: `diff` is sRGB (`colorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`),
the other maps are linear, `nor_gl` is OpenGL-convention. The runtime does not load any of these
yet; they live outside `viewer/` so the Pages deploy stays the size it is.

## Limits

- No map is a photogrammetric or scanned surface. Normal, roughness and height are heuristics from
  image luminance and will not survive close inspection under grazing light.
- Source resolution is the ceiling. A phone photograph of a trunk holds a few hundred texels per
  metre; the manifests give the real figure per tile. The narrow trunk strips from the print are
  flagged as low detail.
- Tiles from small regions repeat their distinctive features several times across 4K. The reuse
  penalty spreads them; it cannot invent new ones.
- The weakest sets are named here so nobody has to discover them: `park_conifer_dapple/bark/bark_dark_furrowed`
  (shaded, underexposed, out of focus; dapple spots partly survive the flattening) and the two narrow
  trunk strips from the framed print and the multi-stemmed oak (about 105 to 125 source pixels wide).
- Sky fits rest on a handful of pixels and a guessed sun azimuth and elevation; the RMS of each
  fit is in `sky.json`.
- Preset numbers are calibrated to the viewer's existing presets, not to measured radiometry.
- The framed print adds the print's own colour, the room light and reflections on the glazing.

## Rights

`oak_lichen_evening`, `ivy_bound_trunk_canopy`, `park_conifer_dapple` and `oak_multistem_sunset` come
from photographs supplied by the repository owner as their own (the last was supplied as two
near-identical frames; the first is used). `aokigahara_moss_print` is a photograph of a
framed print whose printed image's authorship was not stated; confirm the rights before
redistributing that pack publicly. `kamikochi_taisho_dusk` is a watermarked iStock preview
(ID 1191700678, credit PJjaruwan): **no pixel of it is reproduced** anywhere in this repository,
only measurements. Its source file is not committed; a licensed original placed in a directory
passed with `--source-dir`, together with `--include-withheld`, builds its textures locally.
The source photographs carry no GPS or device metadata.

## Regenerating

```bash
pip install numpy pillow scipy
python3 construction_code_v2/gen_asset_packs.py                  # every pack, 4K, about an hour on 4 cores
python3 construction_code_v2/gen_asset_packs.py --pack oak_lichen_evening --size 1024   # quick check
python3 construction_code_v2/gen_asset_packs.py --preview        # region-check sheets under work/
```

Regions, camera and sun estimates are edited in `construction_code_v2/asset_packs_config.json`;
the config's SHA-256 is recorded in every manifest.

Verify a checkout the same way the Pass-2 handover is verified:

```bash
python3 tools/verify_asset_packs.py          # checksums, sizes, pixel dimensions, seamless wrap
python3 tools/verify_asset_packs.py --quick  # the same without the SHA-256 pass
```
